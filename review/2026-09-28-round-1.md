## Pass D: the product critic

- **Does the persona push back?** There is no persona. There is no prompt, no persona goal,
  no hidden objection and no stop condition (`src/scenarios/schema.ts:6-15` has only
  id/title/summary/name/role/opening_line). What exists is six hand-written scripts. In them,
  every retry ends in a tidy win: "Fine. First part by 4pm", "Deal.", "Thanks for being straight
  with me." Two of the three retries win with the same move, "which should slip, X or Y?"
  (`decline-extra-project.yaml:48`, `mid-sprint-scope-change.yaml:48`). The personas fold on
  cue. That is the opposite of the "someone who pushes back" pitch.
- **Would a first-time user freeze at L1?** They can't freeze. They never speak. Today's first
  run is watching your own words type themselves under a status that says **"Your turn"**
  (D-014). It is a reading exercise, not practice.
- **Do the scores feel earned?** No. Every retry jumps +8 to +10 out of 16 in one attempt
  (6→14, 6→15, 5→15). No real person improves like that, and a judge won't believe it. There
  are no rubric descriptors or framework anchors for a score to be earned against (D-012). The
  4/4 clarity "Try saying" is a near-copy of the line it just praised
  (`pr-blocking-release.yaml:56` vs `:58`), so the coaching reads as filler (D-015).
- **Is the paywall resented?** There is no paywall to resent. The plan is a design risk,
  though. "Free: 3 graded sessions" (CLAUDE.md:33) runs out after scenario 1 plus one retry plus
  scenario 2, so the wall lands on **Retry**, the hero action (D-020).
- **Is it boring?** In its current form, yes. Tap, watch 15 seconds of text crawl, read a card.
  The one genuinely good artefact is the scorecard layout: before→after per dimension, quote
  cards and a "Try saying" line. It is the right shape for the product. Nothing behind it is
  real yet.

## Single largest point loss

**C1: there is no real voice path and no real grader** (D-002, D-003, D-004). HardTalk is a
voice product, and as committed it cannot put one real spoken word, one graded quote or one
earned delta on screen. That empties 0:20–1:45 of the video and caps C1 at 1. Recovery needs
three things. First, the Phase 2 grader behind `/server` with a runtime evidence-substring
gate. Second, the Phase 3 ElevenLabs provider with token minting. Third, one uncut L2 take on
a development build on a real device, where the scorecard's quotes are substrings of that
live transcript.
