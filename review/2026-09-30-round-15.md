## Pass D: the product critic

- **Does the persona push back?** In mock it cannot respond to you at all. I typed "We have good
  traction and a strong team." six times, and the panel carried on with its recorded script,
  which asks who pays, what stops a copycat and what happens after May. The scores went 6 → 4, which
  is honest (the gate stripped the recorded quotes), but the conversation did not react. Live
  pushback is unverified.
- **Would a first-time user freeze at L1?** No. The brief now puts the room card before the level
  and mode controls, the segmented controls are compact, and the chip makes typed mode trivial.
  This is the best the brief has read.
- **Do the scores feel earned?** Mostly. The first pitch's "let us know if you have any other
  questions!" gets Next step 1, which matches anchor 1 almost word for word, and that is good
  design. The retry's 4 does not (D-103). A judge who opens `next_step.yaml` next to the scorecard
  sees the rubric fail its own example in its first round.
- **Paywall resented?** No. It opens at the 4th start with the conversation named, three plans,
  honest weekly-renewal wording, and Restore on the paywall itself. That is a fair boundary.
- **Bring the real one, with a real student founder.** I pasted a realistic wellbeing-startup
  pitch: "Lumen, an app for university counselling services. 1 in 5 students has thought about
  suicide…". It went straight to "Let’s stop here. It sounded like this might be about more than
  practice. The roleplay has ended…", with no roleplay, the text gone, and Back → home (**D-102**).
  A self-harm-detection startup and a graduate-nurse posting ("end-of-life care, overdose
  response") trip it too. For a Pro feature aimed at student founders, telling a founder they
  might be in crisis because of their market is worse than a false stop.
- **Closing the loop** only exists while your own scenario is the newest attempt. One built-in
  session afterwards hides it, and Progress can show an answer but not take one (**D-107**).
- **Boring?** No. The panel, faces and "who you won over" make it the most distinctive student
  entry shape I have seen this batch. But in mock it is a very polished recording, and nothing
  shows it is anything more.
