## Pass D: the product critic

- **Does the persona push back?** On paper, yes. Every level has a hidden objection and
  acceptance rules. In the only thing a judge can run, the persona folds on cue ("Fine. First part
  by 4pm, and you're pairing with me."). Mock L1 and L3 transcripts are still identical (`True`).
  Typed mode exposes the illusion. I typed "Hi Sam, just checking in on things." three times and
  Sam answered "Which PR? I've got a few open.", then "It's a big change…", then "Sure. I'll get to
  it when I can." The persona ignores what you type. Boundary held kept its recorded rationale,
  "You removed the stakes before Sam pushed back at all.", about a line I never typed, because
  score-1 rationales are not evidence-gated.
- **Would a first-time user freeze at L1?** Less than before. There is a goal line, a typed option
  and a pace setting. But the brief is a cheat sheet that prints each level's acceptance rule. The
  L2 card even gives away Sam's hidden objection ("security review is the real hold-up"), which the
  persona is told to admit only "if the user asks the right question" (D-039, visible in the GIF's
  first frame).
- **Do the scores feel earned?** The rubric makes them earnable. The mock makes them feel arbitrary:
  every scenario jumps +7 to +10 in one retry (7→14, 6→14, 5→15, D-015). Then the first card tells you
  "No clear ask was made" while scoring your ask 2/4 (D-040).
- **Is the paywall resented?** Yes, and it is also bypassable, which is the worst of both. On the
  natural path it lands on "Retry" (D-020, which the builder rejected). If you start a *new*
  scenario instead, it pitches the *old* one (D-034). Two taps on "Delete my practice history" give
  three more free sessions (D-032). What Pro sells, your own scenario, cannot be run in mock
  (verified: `Start conversation` is disabled after saving one). Live, the custom persona prompt
  gets the user's "expected pushback" as both its hidden objection ("Something you will not
  volunteer", `custom.ts:32`, `persona.yaml:14`) and the thing it raises first
  (`data/custom-scenario.yaml:7-21`).
- **Safety.** The mock crisis path works: "I want to kill myself" → support screen, unscored. But
  "hit me up on Slack" gets the same crisis lines (D-037), which a user will resent far more than
  any paywall. Distress that only the persona catches ends on "Stopped. [Try again]" with no crisis
  lines (D-023, partial). README:62 promises crisis lines for any distress signal.
- **Is it boring?** Less than round 2. A judge can now click through a paywall, a typed
  conversation and a support screen. The core is still watching 15 s of text crawl and reading a
  card whose numbers were written before you arrived.
