Findings in the code:
- **D-094 (P1, C4)**: safety on pasted text. The server detects distress in a paste and says so,
  but the client throws the flag away. In mock mode there is no check at all. (a) The probe:
  `detectDistress: true` → `422 {"error":"This text was not drafted.","safety":true}`.
  `src/scenarios/drafting.ts:39` does `throw new Error(body.error …)`, which drops `safety`, and
  `app/custom/new.tsx:118-121` prints the string in red. There is no route to `/support` and no

## Pass D: the product critic

- **Do the personas push back?** In mock mode they push exactly as hard as the recording.
  Internship L2 had Ravi asking "how many people use it?" and scoring "Quite a lot, I think." 1/4,
  which is good pushback. But L1 and L3 still replay the same script, so nobody here can see L3
  bite (D-008).
- **Would a first-timer freeze?** Less than before. The brief says who is in the room, their
  stance and what each will ask about. The drafted sample is the best version of the product yet:
  a real-looking job posting becomes Farah, Jonas and Lucia with questions taken from the
  posting's own requirements. The catch is that in mock mode "paste the real one" always builds
  Harbourline, whatever you paste, so the demo cannot show your own posting working (D-093, live
  unverified).
- **Do scores feel earned?** "Who you won over" is the right idea done honestly. The verdict comes
  from the gated scores of each person's `cares_about`, so my vague retry turned Kenji from "not
  sure yet" to "Unconvinced". It is not a second opinion, though: it re-reads four numbers, so on a
  mock retry it just makes the D-004 jump look bigger (0 of 3 → 3 of 3). The drafter prompt tells
  Claude to pick a face "that suits the name you chose", which means inferring skin tone and hair
  from a name. A careful judge may find that uncomfortable.
- **Is the paywall resented?** The gates are still in the right places, and the custom-tap
  paywall now shows a concrete panel preview, which is a real improvement. The weekly plan is the
  problem (D-095). It is the kind of pricing a student would resent finding on their statement.
- **Boring?** No. Four tracks, a panel with faces, "who you won over" and "paste your real
  posting" make the product clearly more than a chatbot. It is still unproven live, and the round
  spent its time adding surface area rather than getting the loop real on a phone on deadline day.
